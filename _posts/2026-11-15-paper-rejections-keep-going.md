Academic homepages and CVs suffer from survivor bias: they only list papers after they cross the finish line. When you read someone's publication list, you never see the early rejects, the Round 2 heartbreaks, or the revisions that still fell short.

I recently pulled my submission history from HotCRP and EasyChair. Looking at all the rejections stacked together told a much truer story of what doing systems and security research actually looks like.

Below is my log of **21 conference rejections** (excluding withdrawals and unfinished drafts), grouped into my **Master's** and **Ph.D.** research stages. Paper titles are intentionally omitted—which specific manuscript got rejected matters far less than recognizing how routine, repeated, and survivable rejection is.

## Why I Keep This List: Don't Give Up

A few things stand out to me whenever I re-read these rows:

1. **Close doesn't guarantee an accept—and harsh doesn't mean hopeless.** Early in my Ph.D., a submission drew an *Accept* (`5`) and *Minor revision* (`4`), went through *Major Revision* twice across back-to-back cycles, and still came back *Reject & Resubmit*. Years later, another project walked into `2 / 2 / 2`, then `1 / 1 / 1`, then a *Major-Revision* round rejection before finally finding its home.
2. **Rejections don't stop when you "get better."** Notice the dates: there are just as many rejections in 2023–2026 as in 2016–2020. Pushing into harder problems or unfamiliar communities naturally resets the scoreboard.
3. **Every row above forced the work to grow.** Peer review is noisy, and every researcher has stories of frustrating reviews. Yet almost every painful rejection in these two tables exposed a real blind spot—an unstated assumption, a fragile baseline, a missing experiment, or a muddled story—that, once fixed, turned a borderline draft into solid work I could stand behind for years.

A rejection notification is a verdict on one version of a manuscript on one deadline—not a verdict on the core idea, and never a verdict on you.

If you are a graduate student staring at HotCRP or EasyChair tonight—whether it is your very first reject, a `1 / 1 / 1` that stings, or a *Major Revision* that still didn't make it—close the tab for a day, take what is useful from the reviews, rebuild what is weak, and send it out again. **Don't give up.**
